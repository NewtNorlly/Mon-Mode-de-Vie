Friday. I mainly did three things today: a full fine-tuning of MD file formatting, an extreme marathon of punctuation normalization, and finishing the answer sets for two macroeconomics assignments.

📋 MD Formatting Fine-Tuning

It started from wanting to unify the multiple-choice answer format across all MD files. The old labels in the homework answers — "解析:" (explanation) and "答案：X" (answer: X) — felt redundant, so I decided to cut them all out and instead put an emoji check mark ✅ after the correct option, clean and intuitive.

Two rules:

  • In homework answer files, remove all "解析:" labels; the explanation text goes directly after the option
  • In all non-pure-exercise MD files, add ✅ after the correct option in multiple-choice questions, and no longer write "答案：X"

I modified four files: Homework 1 answers, Homework 2 answers, Homework 3 answers (no multiple-choice, no changes), and the knowledge-point list. All 16 multiple-choice questions in Homework 1 and 2 answers were done, and I'm quite happy with the result — Homework 2 answers especially, which looks clean at a glance.

🔤 The Great Chinese Punctuation Marathon

I thought the formatting fine-tuning would be the end of it, but then I glanced at the files and found a huge number of English half-width commas in the Chinese body text. Unacceptable.

So began a marathon of paragraph-by-paragraph manual replacement:

  • Homework 2 answers: all multiple-choice and review questions done paragraph by paragraph
  • Knowledge-point list: English commas → Chinese commas in all Chinese paragraphs
  • Fan Shide's lecture notes (2193 lines): the whole file done paragraph by paragraph, from GDP accounting to the Solow model, not a single comma spared
  • Homework 4 and 5 exercises: the few spots there were also fixed

The heaviest was Mankiw Macroeconomics Exercises.md (4369 lines). 14 chapters of translated text, with an extremely high density of English commas in the Chinese body. The sentence pattern "在本章中,学生应理解" ("in this chapter, students should understand") repeated 14 times. I pushed through and finished the whole file paragraph by paragraph, keeping the half-width punctuation in formulas ($...$) and numbers untouched.

What I learned: zero tolerance for English punctuation in Chinese body text is a typography principle worth sticking to. From now on, the first thing when writing a file's first chapter is to confirm the input method is in Chinese mode.

Each of Mankiw's 14 chapters has 6 "quick multiple-choice" questions, and previously the correct answers were marked with a yellow highlight <span style="background:#fff88f">. I replaced them all with a ✅ after the correct option, leaving not a single yellow tag.

Zwei Regeln:
